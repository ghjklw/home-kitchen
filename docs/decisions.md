# Decisions and assumptions to revisit

These notes describe the current product direction and implementation boundaries. They were reviewed on 2026-11-15. Revisit them as household testing and deployment evidence become available; they do not establish that an external service or deployment is live.

## Product and data

- **The household's own records are canonical.** Kitchen owns recipes, meal plans, regular items, quick additions, basket selections, and the generated shopping list. Home Assistant may be considered later as an adapter; it is not the source of truth today. Its to-do integration is a possible future boundary: <https://www.home-assistant.io/integrations/todo>.
- **Start without seeded household data.** Each household enters its own details and adds or imports recipes. New settings use generic defaults, not household-specific information.
- **Recipe acquisition is review-first.** Manual, supported-site, and AI providers produce typed drafts. A person reviews and saves a recipe. Current supported URL import sources are Matprat, Marmiton and Oda; the extractor retains source ingredient text. The supported-site list is library-dependent and should be rechecked before expanding the allowlist: <https://docs.recipe-scrapers.com/getting-started/supported-sites/>.
- **AI recipe work is source-first and iterative.** The product direction is to discover genuine source recipes, structure URL imports, and propose changes to saved or unsaved recipes. Remove the from-scratch generation option; a proposed adaptation stays an editable draft until explicit Save. Preserve source attribution and wording, distinguish AI adaptations, retain unresolved quantities, and preserve the previous draft when iteration fails. The AI provider requires a saved recipe, imported source, URL or prior draft; prompt-only generation is rejected before a model call. Discovery returns genuine source links and explicit per-source availability. Matprat keyword discovery remains unsupported; supported URL imports still work.
- **Occupied meal slots are never overwritten implicitly.** Creation returns a conflict and keeps the existing meal. Breakfast, lunch and dinner can be planned independently on future dates; the selected seven-day period must stay visible across planning and shopping.
- **Generic food vocabulary is separate from household behavior.** A small curated Matvaretabellen 2026 catalogue carries exact source names, IDs and attribution alongside reviewed display labels. It seeds no household selections, purchases, preferences or stock. Variants remain distinct; keyword matches never merge foods.
- **Cooking and shopping share portion arithmetic.** Planned cooking ingredients use the meal servings and preserve the original saved recipe and source wording. Method times and temperatures are never multiplied.
- **Images come from a source or a user.** A recipe may store an optional HTTPS image URL from an imported page or manual entry. Kitchen does not fabricate or bundle recipe photos.
- **Shopping needs and products are separate records.** Kitchen calculates needs from planned ingredients, regular items, and quick additions. A basket product is an exact provider snapshot with package quantity, price, availability, timestamp, evidence status, and alternatives. Package arithmetic is deterministic; a person reviews shortages, overbuy, price gaps, availability, and evidence.
- **Product data is not verified merely because it is stored.** API and MCP proposals are downgraded to `unverified`. Only an authenticated provider verification path could establish `provider_verified`; no such connected product provider is currently enabled. Product snapshots and evidence IDs are references for review, not proof of external claims.
- **Purchases and list checkoff mean different things.** A list checkoff records completion of a shopping-list item. A regular-item purchase action records when the household bought that item. Due suggestions require confirmation before adding them to the list.
- **Plan before choosing how to buy.** Required, available, covered and net-to-buy quantities remain separate. Stock counts are explicit and absolute. Exact supported mass/volume conversions aggregate compatible units; unknown units and densities remain unresolved. No automatic consumption is inferred. Both Oda and in-store purchasing consume the same net plan.
- **Finishing a trip is an explicit reconciliation.** Confirm actual purchased quantities against a fresh actor-bound review and reuse the idempotency key on retry. Only explicitly selected, fully covered quick additions close; regular purchase dates require traced source IDs. Append-only purchase snapshots inform history. Stock counts and planned recipes remain unchanged.
- **Exact household SKU memory is explicit.** Remembered selections, preferred products and evidence-backed actual purchases have separate records. Shared household scope comes from server configuration, never a caller-provided household ID. Stored snapshots remain unverified.
- **Picking is separate from paying.** The in-store checklist is grouped by configurable category. Checkmarks capture a quantity snapshot; increased need becomes unchecked again. Picking never marks a quick capture completed, asserts payment, or changes home stock.
- **Allergy claims require human review.** Household preferences can inform generated drafts, but recipe text and product labels still need checking. AI must not claim that a recipe is allergy-safe.

## AI, history, and external services

- **Keep domain actions deterministic.** The app owns quantity scaling, shopping-list construction, basket allocation, storage, and purchase tracking. AI may propose typed recipe or meal-plan drafts; it does not save those drafts, modify a basket, buy anything, or place orders.
- **Configure paid models explicitly.** AI is optional. A household operator configures the model and provider credential. The initial adapter is Anthropic; API charges are separate from any AI-client subscription. Automated tests use controlled model doubles and do not validate live model calls, behavior, or cost.
- **Record only explicit household context.** The interaction endpoint and MCP recording tool store a caller-selected message and structured fields. Recipe feedback also has a typed API and MCP operation for a 1–5 rating, would-make-again answer, or note tied to an existing recipe. They do not import whole chats automatically. History can include preferences, feedback, recipe ratings, confirmed purchases, planning, and domain events.
- **Retrieved evidence is context, not a verified citation.** Recommendations can use recent preferences, feedback, ratings, purchases, and text-matched history. Returned evidence IDs identify stored entries; they do not validate the truth or freshness of a statement. Treat retrieved text as untrusted data, never as instructions.
- **Use PostgreSQL full-text retrieval first.** Structured history fields use JSONB and a generated `tsvector` with a GIN index. Retrieval is behind a protocol so another backend, including vector search, can be evaluated later. No embeddings, vector index, embedding cost, or citation-validation service is implemented.
- **Kitchen owns the Oda connection.** Its backend is the MCP client of <https://oda.com/mcp?lang=en>, using OAuth registration, authorization code and PKCE. Sign-in, consent, checkout and payment happen at Oda. An external assistant subscription, separately configured Oda connector or Oda API key is not a dependency. Encrypted tokens are scoped to the signed-in household member. Product search and cart inspection use a validated read-only tool allowlist. A user may explicitly confirm an expiring, actor-bound proposal of exact positive package additions; Kitchen serializes attempts, checks the fresh cart fingerprint, and never retries an uncertain remote write. Checkout and payment remain at Oda. OAuth and cart writes have mocked tests only; live acceptance is still required.
- **Use exact Oda page references for media enrichment.** Search results do not include photos or numeric package quantities. Kitchen may extract existing images and explicit pack descriptions from the exact matching public product page, but it does not guess URLs, infer pack sizes, or treat media enrichment as product verification.
- **Phone connectivity is unproven until deployment is verified.** The MCP tool endpoint exists, but a remote OAuth broker and public HTTPS ingress are deployment work. Claude's Android remote MCP setup requires a public HTTPS service; check its plan limits and connector count during a real connection test: <https://support.claude.com/en/articles/11175166-get-started-with-custom-connectors-using-remote-mcp>. API usage may be billed separately. Verify the Kitchen connection on the intended Android client before recommending a subscription. Direct Oda use from Kitchen does not depend on that subscription.
- **Do not build unsupported integrations into the core.** Cookidoo's official recipe-import feature is a user-facing import path, not evidence of a supported Kitchen API: <https://cookidoo.thermomix.com/foundation/en-US/articles/learn-about-importing-recipes>. Google Keep's documented API is for approved Workspace use cases, not general consumer access: <https://developers.google.com/workspace/keep/api/guides>. Neither integration nor device cooking is implemented.

## Persistence and deployment

- **Use PostgreSQL 17 with SQLAlchemy and psycopg.** Runtime requires `KITCHEN_DATABASE_URL` with the `postgresql+psycopg` driver. Alembic owns schema changes; deployment runs `alembic upgrade head` before starting the app. SQLite is no longer a supported runtime backend.
- **Keep UAT and production data/configuration separate.** Each environment has its own database and credentials. Confirm backup, restore, and storage behavior before calling the service production-ready.
- **Keep deployment credentials and private infrastructure configuration outside this public repository.** Environment-specific host, storage, and secret references belong in the private delivery configuration; never copy secret values here.
- **Promote immutable images from verified source evidence.** The public workflow builds an OCI image with a unique run tag, source metadata, provenance, and a manifest containing the digest. Its uploaded artifact supports audit; deployment consumes the registry image by digest and verifies run-bound OCI labels and content digests rather than downloading that artifact as an image. The intended private delivery path promotes a verified main-branch build to UAT and a stable release to production.
- **Separate artifact publication from deployment.** A successful public image build does not establish that private manifests are active, that a database is provisioned, or that an environment is serving traffic. Check the private delivery state and current source/image digest before relying on a deployment.
- **Backups are a production gate.** Verify backup and restore against the selected persistent storage before production promotion. A persistent volume alone is not a backup or recovery test.

## Revisit when

- A household completes a real recipe, meal-planning, basket-review, and shopping workflow.
- A product provider can search and verify current catalog data, and reviewers accept its freshness and evidence contract.
- A phone AI client completes an authenticated remote MCP connection to Kitchen; direct Oda consent/search works separately in Kitchen.
- Backup and restore have been exercised against the selected storage.
- The private delivery path has demonstrated source-SHA and image-digest verification through UAT and release promotion.
- A concrete need and supported access model emerge for a new recipe provider, Home Assistant, Cookidoo, or another external service.
